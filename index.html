      📸 Day by day

      The whole story

      Every stop, every hike, plus the trivia you’ll be reciting at dinner. Tap a photo to see it big.

      Day 1 · Friday, November 13

      Desert Highway & Lajitas Oasis

      • 7:00 AM
        Wheels up — drive & check-in

        Hit the open road heading deep into the Chihuahuan Desert toward Lajitas Golf Resort, a historic resort nestled right along the Rio Grande.

      • Evening
        Dinner at Lajitas Golf Resort Restaurant

        Settle into the desert atmosphere with a great meal right on site.

      🤠 Fun fact Lajitas once elected a beer-drinking pet goat named Clay Henry III as its honorary mayor. Its championship golf course, Black Jack’s Crossing, was designed by PGA legend Lanny Wadkins.

      Day 2 · Saturday, November 14

      Ghost Town Morning & Chisos Basin Basecamp

      • Morning
        Breakfast at Venga Cafe

        Kick off the morning with local coffee, fresh pastries and breakfast tacos in the historic Terlingua Ghost Town.

      • 1:00 PM
        Camp check-in at Chisos Basin Campground

        Head up into Big Bend National Park and claim the site. Fill every container you own before you leave the basin — there is no water at camp.

      • 3:45 PM
        Window Trail hike

        Lace up your boots for the classic basin warm-up hike out to the pour-off at the Window.

      • 7:30 PM
        Dinner in the basin

        A hearty dinner with a side of incredible night sky right in the Chisos Basin.

      🤠 Fun fact Terlingua was once one of the largest quicksilver (mercury) mining communities in the world during the early 20th century. Meanwhile, the Chisos Basin is the only mountain basin enclosed entirely within a national park in the United States.

      Day 3 · Sunday, November 15 · the big one

      The Crown Jewel: South Rim Expedition

      • 7:00 AM
        Early breakfast at Chisos Basin

        Fuel up in the dark. This is a long day on the trail.

      • 8:30 AM
        Start the South Rim Trail hike

        Water, layers, electrolytes, and every snack you own. It gets cold and windy at the top even when the basin is warm.

      • Afternoon
        Return to camp — rest, then dinner

        Shoes off, feet up, then dinner under some of the darkest night skies in North America.

      🤠 Fun fact The South Rim sits at roughly 7,200 feet, offering a dramatic precipice where hikers look nearly 5,000 vertical feet straight down across the Rio Grande into Mexico.

      Day 4 · Monday, November 16

      High-Altitude Views & Historic Marathon

      • 7:00 AM
        Early start — pack up camp

        Coffee, tear down, double-check the site for anything left behind. Leave No Trace, and don’t leave crumbs for the javelinas.

      • 9:00 AM
        Lost Mine Trail hike

        Back at the trailhead by noon. Big views for a relatively short hike — the best payoff-per-mile in the park.

      • 1:00 PM
        Lunch at DB’s Rustic Iron BBQ

        Mouth-watering brisket, ribs and West Texas barbecue out of a trailer in Terlingua. You’ve earned it.

      • Afternoon
        Scenic drive to Marathon

        Take the long way if the light is good — this is the drive people write songs about.

      • 4:00 PM
        Check in at the Gage Hotel

        Shower, real bed, air conditioning. Civilization, briefly.

      • 7:30 PM
        Dinner at the Gage Hotel

        A proper dinner on property to close out the trail days.

      🤠 Fun fact The Lost Mine Trail is named after a legendary Spanish silver mine rumored to have been hidden by enslaved laborers who staged a historic revolt. Meanwhile, the Gage Hotel was built in 1927 by Alfred S. Gage, a cattle baron who owned over half a million acres of West Texas ranch land.

      Day 5 · Tuesday, November 17

      Grand Finale & Homeward Bound

      • 9:00 AM
        Breakfast at the Gage Hotel

        Savor a relaxed breakfast before pointing the car toward home.

      • Late morning → evening
        The drive back — and the big decision

        The long road (about 3.5 hours to Marathon) is probably the most scenic drive in Texas. The shorter road (about 2.25 hours) is still nice. According to Gemini: “Yes, the extra 1.5 hours is absolutely worth it if you have the time and enjoy high-reward scenic driving.”

      🤠 Fun fact Marathon acts as the quiet, artistic gateway to Big Bend and is officially designated a Dark Sky Community, boasting some of the clearest stargazing conditions in the country.
